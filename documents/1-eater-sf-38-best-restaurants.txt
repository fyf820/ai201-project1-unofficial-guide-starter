The 38 Best Restaurants in San Francisco
The essential restaurants that define San Francisco, according to Eater editors

by Dianne de Guzman
Updated Apr 10, 2026, 3:27 PM PDT



Dianne de Guzman
Dianne de Guzman is the regional editor for Eater’s Northern California/Pacific Northwest sites, writing about restaurant and bar trends, upcoming openings, and pop-ups for the San Francisco Bay Area, Portland, and Seattle.
San Francisco is a vast city. There are as many small climes as there are micro neighborhoods, as many bizarro landmarks as there are world famous vistas. That same thinking goes for restaurants. The Eater 38 is the ultimate guide to restaurants that define a city’s dining scene. Take it from the Eater editors who are out at all those restaurants, bars, and cafes: These are San Francisco’s top places to eat.

In this latest refresh, write-ups include even more relevant info for diners, including a rough range of pricing for each destination. One $ is shorthand for inexpensive places where entrees are mostly under $15; $$ designates spots where entrees are mostly under $30; $$$ means entrees are mostly under $40; $$$$ indicates restaurants with pricier entrees or a tasting menu experience.

New to the map in this spring 2026 update: halal barbecue and Indonesian food specialist Fikscue, Korean American cafe Sōhn, and Tuscan fine dining spot Via Aurelia.

To be considered for the Eater 38, restaurants must be open for at least six months. For the most exciting new restaurants in town, check out the heatmap.

Abacá
Open for: Weekday breakfast, weekend brunch, and dinner

Price range: $$

When Francis Ang opened the doors to his Kimpton Alton Hotel restaurant in Fisherman’s Wharf, the city already knew it was the breath of fresh seabreeze air diners needed. In short order his Pinoy heritage cooking drew eaters to the otherwise-for-tourists neighborhood: sisig fried rice with poached egg and pickled onions, adobo-glazed yuba skin barbecue skewer, American wagyu beef pares with parsnip and soy beef jus. Ang was a pop-up phenom before rolling out the chic bar and restaurant in 2021. The vibe is fancy but accessible; this is not a suit-and-tie restaurant, though that wouldn’t be overdressing, either. Breakfast here is geared to tourists — though a $6 bibingka rice cake with caramelized brie and salted egg is awesome any day — whereas brunch is a San Francisco-wide weekend affair. Reservations are bookable here.

Best for: A booming dining tour through the Philippines with a big group.

Butter & Crumble
Open for: Pastries

When the line goes beyond crawling around the block to literally circumnavigating the block, it’s important to capture the moment. Butter & Crumble is essential to this moment in San Francisco. The bakery is home to giga-viral croissants and treats including bacon, egg, and cheese and pistachio and cardamom sugar croissants. The small shop offers coffee, too, on a pink La Marzoco from a team in pink aprons. As founder Sophie Smith readies for a second shop in North Beach, there’s still time to say you spent three hours getting one of the OGs.

Best for: Camping out early (the bakery opens at 9 a.m.) to try one of the rotating hits; in January, look for the banana praline cruffin.

Dalida
Open for: Brunch, lunch, and dinner

Price range: $$$

Any San Francisco naysayers can take a little jaunt into the Presidio and find their jaws somewhere on the floor, back in the grass in Tunnel Tops Park. Outside of existing in as spectacular a location as possible, Dalida is simply put one of the most supreme restaurants in California. Husband-and-wife chefs Laura and Sayat Ozyilmaz say the federal land feels like a slice of the Mediterranean, ideal for showcasing their modern riffs on the cuisine. Aromatic and juicy lamb shoulder tandoor and sea urchin tahdig topped with smoked trout roe battle for main supremacy; the smart diner springs for both. For dessert, try the tavuk gogsu brulee, also known as Turkish milk pudding made with shredded chicken breast.

Best for: Refreshing your “San Francisco really is that wonderful” reserves any time they’re running low, ideally as a sunny evening sunsets into a classy dinner.

Golden Boy Pizza
Open for: Lunch, dinner

Price range: $

San Francisco is absolutely swarming with great pizzerias whether you favor a floppy Neapolitan pie from Tony’s or thick Detroit-style slabs from Square Pie Guys. But Golden Boy remains an institution for good reason: It’s a San Francisco pastime to stand on the sidewalk under that glowing neon sign waiting for your pan or slice while soaking in all that is the North Beach neighborhood. There are classic options, including cheese, pepperoni, and sausage — but the pro move is to get the clam and garlic pie. It’s a zingy, briny, bready meal built on a doughy base with a sturdy bottom. Take your pizza to nearby Washington Square Park if the weather allows.

If you drive: North Beach can be a difficult neighborhood to park in, especially given neighboring apartment buildings and parklets which take up some of the area’s parking spaces. Give yourself extra time to locate that perfect spot or, even better, take public transportation to this corner and save yourself some frustration.

Quince
Open for: Lunch on Fridays and Saturdays, dinner

Price range: $$$$

Fine dining is known for its hospitality, but truthfully, some higher-end places can feel stuffy and tense. That’s thankfully not the case at three-Michelin-starred Quince, which underwent a renovation and reopened in November 2023; if anything, the updates to the dining room make the environment feel comfortable and inviting. That’s not to mention the masterful food of chef Michael Tusk, which easily shifts with the seasons, thanks to farm partners Fresh Run Farm. Diners will also want to indulge in an offering from the restaurant’s vintage amaro cart when it’s wheeled by after the entrees if the wine pairing doesn’t already have you floating out the door. You’ll feel well taken care of by the night’s end.

Best for: Elegant, celebratory meals or the Michelin Guide aficionados (or Michelin-curious folks) in the group chat.

Lunette
Open for: Lunch, dinner

Price range: $

Chef Nite Yun is one of the San Francisco Bay Area’s most singular cooks. There’s her love of black pepper, incorporating the spice “by the handful” as Cesar Hernandez writes for the San Francisco Chronicle. There’s her storied past — including the ascent and decline of her first restaurant Nyum Bai amongst her tales — captured lovingly by Netflix’s Chef’s Table: Noodles. The accolades, the myths, don’t matter. Lunette’s well-priced, crunchy, chewy, and spicy menu is some of the best food in the city, and certainly at the Ferry Building and along the waterfront. The Khmer Fried Chicken Wings, lightly battered in rice flour and served with a lime, are one of the smartest ways to spend $16. It’s a run don’t walk situation.

Best for: Rolling through on a weekday — preferably a sunny one to take advantage of the outdoor seating at the Ferry Building — to catch up with a friend over a mind-blowing testament to Cambodian cuisine.

Four Kings
Open for: Dinner

Price range: $$$

Sometimes a restaurant’s origin story is so well-written one would think Stan Lee put it all down in stardust like just another superhero coming of age. Such is the case for Chinatown’s Four Kings, the first restaurant for chefs Franky Ho and Michael Long who met in the kitchen at Mister Jiu’s over their shared Cantonese lineage. Chinese sausage and bacon clay pot rice with house-made bacon and sausage. Saucy and spicy mapo spaghetti. A phenomenal fried squab to make James Beard blush. That’s all after the free roasted peanuts and smashed cucumbers hit the table when guests sit down.

Know before you go: Reservations can be difficult, as the restaurant has maintained its buzziness since opening in 2024, but there are still some ways to get in. Join the pre-opening line to snag a table, or try to score a walk-in closer to closing once the hopeful crowds have died down (although fair warning: some dishes may be sold out).

Turtle Tower
Open for: Lunch, dinner

Price range: $$

The return of Turtle Tower marked a positive news story for San Francisco, as the beloved restaurant came back from closure to a splashy new opening in the Financial District. Back are the beloved pho bowls, including the crowd favorite pho ga, plus newer items like the pandan mochi waffles. There’s also now a cocktail menu, in case you want a harder drink with your meal, such as 18 Kings, a coconut fat-washed Japanese whiskey drink made with Thai tea.

Best for: A hearty, warming lunch in FiDi, or a late-night bite, as the restaurant stays open until 3 a.m. on Fridays and Saturdays.

House of Prime Rib
Open for: Dinner

Price range: $$$

This 70-year-old Nob Hill classic is a trip back to a time when plating with tweezers was not yet a thing. The name says it all: House of Prime Rib serves one thing and one thing only, and it does that roast prime rib very, very well. The only choices to make are: meat temperature, cut thickness, mashed or loaded baked potatoes, and martini or Manhattan. Each plate comes with a salad (prepared tableside), creamed spinach, Yorkshire pudding, and potatoes — and, of course, a hulking piece of beef cut from one of the wheeled meat carts.

Know before you go: Reservations are highly recommended when going to House of Prime Rib (especially if you’re in a group), but if you’re out of luck and have a party of two, it’s not impossible to get a walk-in seat at the bar.

Mandalay Restaurant
Open for: Dinner, Lunch (weekends only)

Price range: $$

Though it isn’t the flashiest or the most crowded, Mandalay might be best of San Francisco’s Burmese restaurants — certainly it’s the one that makes the least compromises to accommodate Western palates, which in general means bigger, punchier flavors. Whatever you do, don’t skip the best fermented tea leaf salad in town. It’s a rendition of the classic that’s available without lettuce or cabbage to tamp down the deliciously pungent flavors or diminish the outrageous crunch of the nuts and seeds. The restaurant also received the James Beard America’s Classics Award in 2024.

Must-try dish: Try the homey samusa soup or any of the fragrant noodle dishes.

Kin Khao
Open for: Lunch and dinner

Price range: $$

On a recent spring night, Kin Khao remains packed and noisy more than a decade into its run in Union Square. Dishes arrive fast. Mushroom pate on rice crackers is simple and fine enough to attract customers on just its own merit. Then come oily pork cracklings, yaowaraj noodles with spicy tofu. Chef-owner Pim Techamuanvivit maintains the quality and prestige of Kin Khao’s flavors and dishes even while jet-setting around the world and running restaurants in both San Francisco and Bangkok. Order the green rabbit curry with meatballs, with a tin of fluffy brown rice, that hints at deeper levels of spice expanding through the meal while keeping the palette on cruise control.

Best for: Peeking into the origins of modern Thai American cuisine by way of one of the best to ever do it.

The Progress
Open for: Dinner

The work of Stuart Brioza, Nicole Krasinski, and managing partner Elizabeth DePalmer is without exaggeration the stuff of Northern California legend. Their restaurants State Bird Provision, Anchovy Bar, and the Progress are proving grounds for chefs and a testament to the teams built up by Brioza and Krasinski. Best for: Special occasions where nobody wants the vibe to feel too stuffy, where crashing through glitzy glizzies is just as desirable as plates of phenomenal duck.

Breadbelly
Open for: Breakfast, lunch

Price range: $

A stupid take has emerged on rapidly enshittifying social media platforms in 2025: San Francisco cafe culture sucks. Writ large, few bakeries are wonderful enough to double dip as a cafe. Breadbelly is the rare bakery powerful enough to not just serve soba-studded cookies and green kaya buns, but to boast a cafe and parklet charming enough to remind San Franciscans why touching grass is worth logging off. The 50/50 is worth an order, a combo of black tea, condensed milk, and black coffee from (most recently) award-winning Dune Coffee Roasters. Few bakeries and cafes in the Avenues can compete with Breadbelly. And now, thankfully, a second location has opened up at Pier 70.

Best for: A weekday morning coffee and pastry to start the day, ideal to parlay into a leisurely day on Clement Street or hopping in your car to play hooky north of the city.

Outta Sight Pizza
Open for: Lunch, dinner

Price range: $

Outta Sight Pizza gained a solid fanbase for its New York-inspired slices, but the dialed-in sauces and flavor combinations make it a standout from the pizza crowd. That’s thanks to chef Eric Ehler, who left the fine-dining world to take on the more approachable realm of pizza. The shop skews toward thin crust, but there are also some tavern-style pies and granny slices to keep things fresh. Ruby Pie is the shop’s top-seller and it tips its hat to New York’s Rubirosa, combining trails of vodka sauce with marinara and pesto, and hits of mozzarella. Above all else, don’t gloss over the specials — this is where the team gets to play around, and with it comes unexpected touches, such as a recent ginger scallion chicken Caesar salad or a mapo slice inspired by Chinatown neighbors Four Kings.

Vibe check: Outta Sight has big skater vibes — Ehler and much of the team like to go out and hit the streets, skateboard in tow — so it only makes sense that this group turns out some excellent merch. You’ll most likely want to see what they have in stock after your slice.

Cinderella Bakery & Cafe

Though the small enclave of Russian markets and businesses in the Richmond has contracted over the years, Cinderella Bakery & Cafe endures. Kiln
Open for: Dinner

Price range: $$$$

Kiln’s roots lie in another Michelin-starred San Francisco restaurant: John Wesley and Julianna Yang are alums of Sons & Daughters, and together they’ve since branched out, launching their restaurant Kiln in 2023. Wesley runs the kitchen, crafting a tasting menu with minimalist-looking bites and dishes that require plenty of technique and multi-day preparation. The tasting menu changes, but consider the thought put into the cornette bite, composed of smoked beet puree molded into cones, then filled with an emulsified bone marrow paired with huckleberries. It’s just one window into the food world Wesley is creating, which the Michelin Guide recognized with a second star for the restaurant in 2025. Yang, meanwhile, leads the front of house, creating fun moments at the table, such as a Polaroid at the end of the meal for diners to take home as a keepsake.

Vibe check: The restaurant skews towards a minimalist vibe, but it’s worth noting many of the glasses and plateware that are custom-made for the bites, with some refracting the light in playful ways.

Via Aurelia
Open for: Dinner

Price range: $$$

Chef David Nayfeld’s tribute to Tuscan cuisine opened in September 2025 with a bang, debuting in an all-new buildout with beautiful decor and an equally-beautiful view of Oracle Park and the waterfront. Beyond those points, however, is a restaurant that boasts a selection of Tuscan dishes that are perhaps lesser-seen in Bay Area restaurants. Think a spin on sformato, informed by seasonal produce, house-made pastas like tortelli Mugellani, and fennel seed-crusted lamb. Diners can order a la carte or take on the five-course tasting menu that leads diners through the options. Add on a serious wine and cocktail list, and you have the makings of a special night out.

Best for: Upscale dates and friend meetups, special occasions, and business meetings with an expense account.

It’s been written in the most important annals of San Francisco history (read: cookbooks) and believe the hype: Zuni Cafe is the real deal. Jules

This Lower Haight pizzeria is, as Iggy Azalea once put it, the new classic. Rintaro
Open for: Dinner

Price range: $$

The Mission District’s top izakaya is sunken from the sidewalk. It’s wood-paneled, an intimate window into the kitchen plain for anyone in the restaurant to see, not just for those seated at the hinoki cedar bar. It would be a mistake to say it’s a “hidden gem” or any kind of secret, though. Kyoto-born and Northern California-raised chef Sylvan Brackett’s Rintaro has been a sensation for more than a decade, surviving and thriving through the various San Francisco eras. Dining here — whether on the udon “carbonara” with raw egg yolk, Becker Lane Berkshire pork gyoza with chicken foot jelly, or the elegant sashimi — is a Bay Area rite of passage.

Best for: Going in as soon as possible through last-minute reservation notifications, solo at said cedar bar, or for an intimate dinner date.

Fikscue Thrive City
Open for: Lunch, dinner

Price range: $$

Fikscue built its reputation on Texas-style halal barbecue and Indonesian dishes that have captured the adoration of diners at its original Alameda location. In its first year of operation, the restaurant received plenty of acclaim and awards (including a Best New Restaurant nod by Eater in 2024), and owners Fik and Reka Saleh added a second location, this time in the complex surrounding Chase Center. Expect the same dishes that diners clamor for in Alameda, such as beef back ribs, jalapeño-cheese sausage, nasi goreng, and smoked brisket rendang. Capped with the iced dessert drink cendol, with green pandan jelly in coconut milk, it’s a dynamic pairing of cultures you can’t find anywhere else.

Must-try dish: There’s plenty to like at Fikscue, but there’s just something about wielding a dino rib that is not only delicious but feels absolutely primal to hold.

Beit Rima
Open for: Lunch and dinner

Price range: $

Samir Boulos Ishaq Hanna Jiries Abed el Masiah Issa Mughannam took over his father’s burger joint and made it a global destination for Arabic comfort food. Piglet & Co
Open for: Brunch and dinner

Price range: $$

Taiwanese comfort food near you might not mean much to some, but, for those on the take, this is like hearing a Prince concert is on at the dive bar down the street. That’s no exaggeration. Chef Chris Yang and partner Marcelle Gonzales Yang’s cooking has been a gold mine for the city’s diners since their Hén-zhì and El Chino Grande pop-up days. When the restaurant opened in 2023, it became a fast favorite, nabbing national accolades. The move here is the nostalgic and inventive honey walnut shrimp and pork toast, ground white shrimp and pork mousse atop the Japanese milk bread shokupan. The mala barbecue pork ribs are numbing and delectable, and wrapping the meal with the singular tofu pudding, or dou hua, is just plain smart.
