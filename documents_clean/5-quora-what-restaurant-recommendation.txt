What restaurant would you recommend to someone visiting San Francisco?
Gil Silberman
As a visitor…

Many of the downtown waterfront restaurants with great views are actually pretty good. Off the top of my head, Epic Steak, Waterbar, Hog Island Oyster Company, Slanted Door, La Mar, Coqueta, Hillstone, and Greens are all great. For something less formal on the water, Mission Rock Resort, The Ramp, AT Water Tavern, Pier 23, Queens Louisiana, and the Blue Mermaid are all okay. Not much in Fisherman’s Wharf / Pier 39, except Fog Harbor is very good and the Crab House is a fun experience.

There are some excellent food trucks, both mobile and in food truck lots. In late spring to early fall Presidio Picnic on Sundays is one of SF’s epic experiences, a bunch of food trucks with a DJ in a national park setting. SPARK SF, much smaller and somewhat less scenic, is a permanent food park that operates everyday. There are lots of trucks for weekday lunches outside the Salesforce Transit center, and you can take your food up to the gorgeous park on top. On Saturday or Sunday you can visit a farmer’s market and eat the popup food there. My favorite is Primavera on Saturdays at the Ferry Plaza, some of the best Mexican food I’ve ever had. Any other day you can roam the Ferry Building food hall and pick out whatever you want to eat on benches and tables overlooking the water.

If you’re a fine dining tourist then just take your pick of the top 100 restaurants in town, whatever sounds good to you. Or take a food excursion to Napa Valley. That’s a different question, the best or favorite restaurants overall.

You might enjoy a ticketed popup dinner, either a daily or occasional meal served in a non-traditional kitchen / dining setting, could be a secret location or a commercial kitchen, a chef’s home, etc. There are many sources for these. The most famous is Lazy Bear, which can be very hard to get a reservation.

Another special occasion would be a winemaker or brewmaster dinner. These can be expensive but they’re great experiences — google will help you find them, or point you to sites that list upcoming special event dinners.

Too bad Teatro Zinzanni is on hiatus for a decade after losing their lease to the America’s Cup. That’s like a dinner theater, but instead of a musical it’s an indoor circus. The food and entertainment were both first rate. They’re approved to return as an entire hotel-restaurant complex on the waterfront, so if all goes well that may be an option if you’re reading this a few years from now. There are a few other entertainment supperclubs but they were the queen of them. Oh, that reminds me. AsiaSF - a fixed course “gender illusionist” revue with good food. I would say only in SF, but it’s apparently based on something more common in Southeast Asia.

Several well known restaurants with good food are particularly gorgeous, so I would go for the iconic only in San Francisco design. Aside from Waterbar and Epic there’s Farallon, Fog City Diner, and Boulevard, all designed by Pat Kuleto. Farallon is particularly impressive, everything inside from the stair railings to the seats and light fixtures, is all custom designed and handmade. Kokkari is a beautiful non-Kuleto restaurant.

San Francisco doesn’t really have its own unique cuisine, unless you count California cuisine. Other than cioppino (available many places, an informal favorite is at Calzone’s) and some less well known dishes like hangtown fry and celery Victor, and being the birthplace of California pizza, it’s mostly regional cooking from around the world, sometimes with some fusion elements or local fresh vegetables.

Colleen Sollars
Thanks for the A2A. Where do I start? There are so many. I’ll give you two short lists, categorized by expense relative to other SF restaurants, not many of them are actually cheap. These are places I’ve eaten many times with no disappointments.

Less expensive:

Namu Gaji (the SF Ferry Building location) elevated Korean comfort food

Old Islamic Mandarin - Uighur Chinese with fabulous savory pancakes and lamb

Mission Chinese - not your typical Chinese food

Mensho Tokyo - best ramen in SF in my opinion

**One in-between: Universal Cafe - best of California cuisine. Seasonal, fresh, skillful. Go for brunch and expect to wait, worth it!

More expensive:

Slanted Door - upscale Vietnamese food

Swan Oyster Depot - best fresh pile of seafood you’ll find in SF. Piles of crab, shrimp, prawns, scallops, they’ve got it all. It’s not fancy, but it is high quality.

Saison - VERY expensive, but one of the most unique meals you’ll ever have. One of the best meals of my life. Get reservations ahead. They forage many of their ingredients from the area where the protein in the dish comes. This article breaks down what you can expect, and it’s offshoot which is half the price.

It depends on what kind of experience you want. Steak and drinks, I agree House of Prime Rib - there are others.

I don’t do North beach that much anymore - lots of good Italian restaurants all over town though.

Asian - if you want a place popular with local west side asians (and other local residents) with the focus on the food (the aunties making the dumplings in the back, that sort of thing) I like Shandong Deluxe on Taraval.
